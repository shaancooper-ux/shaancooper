01 / Design + build / Feb–Apr 2026

Returning a damaged test stand to hot fire.

As a lead on the rebuild, I helped redesign and restore a damaged liquid bi-propellant test stand. The upgraded system integrates bang-bang pressurization to eliminate regulator droop, improves operator access, and incorporates protection for fluid systems in the event of an engine failure.

The rebuilt stand went on to fire a 2,000 lbf engine on October 16, 2026. Setup improvements reduced preparation time from six hours to four.

02 / System design / Jan–Mar 2026

A bigger capability.
A mobile platform.

I co-led the design of an upgraded feed system intended to support engines producing up to 10,000 lbf of thrust. The architecture combines full cryogenic capability, substantially expanded tank capacity, throttle control, and higher mass-flow capability.

A compact, trailer-mounted layout prioritizes mobility and transportability while accommodating a broader range of engine test campaigns.

03 / Structural analysis

Evaluating the structure behind the test.

I performed finite element analysis in ANSYS Mechanical to assess whether a vertical engine test structure could support full engine loading with a factor-of-two load case.

The work examined structural response to support test-stand design decisions and protection of nearby fluid systems. The image shows the modeled total-deformation result.

ANSYS MechanicalFinite element analysisStructural loading
04 / Analysis + characterization / Aug 2025–Present

Turning test data into system insight.

I develop Python tools to characterize fluid-system and engine behavior, model pressure losses and mass flow, and determine tank pressurization requirements using Venturi flow principles.

Post-hot-fire analysis covers characteristic velocity (C*), specific impulse, thrust coefficient, system pressures, valve states, and propellant temperatures.

The pressure model predicted target chamber pressure within approximately 5 psi before regulator droop.
