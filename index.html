Projects

Design + build / Feb–Apr 2026

Test stand rebuild

Helped lead the redesign and rebuild of a damaged liquid bi-propellant test stand. The upgraded system integrates bang-bang pressurization to eliminate regulator droop and incorporates protection for fluid systems in the event of an engine failure.

The rebuilt stand went on to fire a 2,000 lbf engine on October 16, 2026. Setup improvements reduced preparation time from around five hours to three.
System design / Jan–Mar 2026

Next-generation feed system

I was a lead on the design of a next-generation feed system intended to support engines producing up to 10,000 lbf of thrust—a separate project from the test-stand rebuild. The architecture combines full cryogenic capability, substantially expanded tank capacity, throttle control, and higher mass-flow capability.

A compact, trailer-mounted layout prioritizes mobility and transportability while accommodating a broader range of engine test campaigns.

Structural analysis

Structural analysis

Performed finite element analysis in ANSYS Mechanical to assess whether a vertical engine test structure could support full engine loading with a factor-of-two load case.

The work examined structural response to support test-stand design decisions and protection of nearby fluid systems. The image shows the modeled total-deformation result.

Analysis + characterization / Aug 2025–Present

Hot-fire data analysis

Python analysis of hot-fire test data characterizes fluid-system and engine behavior. The graph shown here displays measured hot-fire data.

Developed a separate set-pressure calculator to model pressure losses and mass flow and determine tank pressurization requirements using Venturi flow principles.

The separate set-pressure calculator predicted target chamber pressure within approximately 5 psi before regulator droop.
