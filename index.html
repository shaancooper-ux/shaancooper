Design + build / Feb–Apr 2026 · Hot-fired Oct 2026

DRACOTest stand rebuild

Was a project lead on the redesign and rebuild of a damaged liquid bi-propellant test stand. The upgraded system integrates bang-bang pressurization to eliminate regulator droop and incorporates protection for fluid systems in the event of an engine failure.

The rebuilt stand went on to fire a 2,000 lbf engine on October 16, 2026. Setup improvements from the work done during the rebuild reduced preparation time from around five hours to three.
Design study — not built / Jan–Mar 2026

MURPHNext-generation feed system

I was a lead on the design of Murph, a next-generation feed system with a design target of supporting engines producing up to 10,000 lbf of thrust. Murph was never built; the work remained a design study, separate from the Draco rebuild.

The proposed trailer-mounted architecture aimed to combine cryogenic capability, expanded tank capacity, throttle control, and higher mass flow in a mobile layout. These were design goals, not demonstrated capabilities.

Structural analysis

Structural analysis

Performed finite element analysis in ANSYS Mechanical to assess whether a vertical engine test structure could support full engine thrust.

The work examined structural response to support test-stand design decisions and protection of nearby fluid systems. The image shows the modeled total-deformation result.

Analysis + characterization / Aug 2025–Present

Hot-fire data analysis

Python analysis of hot-fire test data characterizes fluid-system and engine behavior. The graph shown here displays measured hot-fire data.

Developed a separate set-pressure calculator to model pressure losses and mass flow and determine tank pressurization requirements using Venturi flow principles.

The separate set-pressure calculator predicted target chamber pressure within approximately 5 psi before regulator droop.
